# Lore and mechanics review (Books 1–4 boundary)

This is a source-checked design brief for adapting *Dungeon Crawler Carl* into a small tactics game. Canon claims are paraphrased and linked. Most detailed rule references are secondary fan-maintained databases; the publisher's samples are primary but cover only excerpts. Treat any proposed turn rules, stats, forms, menus, and encounter scripting below as game adaptation unless explicitly marked canon.

## What the lore supports

### Katia and shapechanging

**Canon / secondary-source supported:** Katia is a Doppelgänger. Her race allows her to reshape her body and equipped mass into different shapes, with the same total mass as the original. It does not make her acquire the copied creature's skills or powers. Her ability to make useful shapes depends on practice and spatial imagination; the once-per-30-hour-day limit in the reviewed profile belongs to **Rush**, not to shapechanging itself. Do not transfer that cooldown to Shapeshift. Her Monster Truck Driver class pairs momentum/speed with Constitution-oriented toughness. These details are summarized in [NovelPantheon's Katia profile](https://www.novelpantheon.com/novels/dungeon-crawler-carl/characters/katia) and the [Doppelgänger entry](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.fandom.com/wiki/Doppelg%C3%A4nger). They are not all independently confirmed by the publisher samples.

**Not established as canon:** A selectable roster of named forms with fixed stat packages, unlimited mid-combat switching, or a universally optimal combat shape. Do not present a game's “wolf/bear/tank” menu as a list from the novels.

**Adaptation recommendation:** Offer three or four tactical presets as authored game fiction—e.g. *Compact* (normal footprint), *Braced* (defense), *Long Reach* (attack reach), and *Runner* (movement). Label them “combat shapes” or “forms” as a game interface. Give each a clear mass budget and a tactical tradeoff; mark every stat bonus as a game rule. Avoid copying named novel creatures' signature abilities. If character development includes shape mastery, make it unlock a new legal silhouette or a lower action cost, not a claim that canon assigns those exact stats.

### Levels, stats, skills, and class

**Canon / secondary-source supported:** The System assigns starting stats based on a crawler's pre-dungeon physical and mental profile. Crawler levels come from XP and award three stat points each. Crawlers cannot manually allocate those points until the Third Floor race/class selection; afterwards, point allocation is done from a menu in a safe room. A crawler's skills are tracked separately from crawler level and stats: skills can reflect prior-life knowledge, be learned or improved through use/training, and can be affected by equipment, rewards, race, or class. Classes can grant skills/spells and other perks, and some permit a skill to train above its ordinary cap. See the [DCC Database stats summary](https://www.dccdatabase.com/wiki/player_stats/), [skills index](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.fandom.com/wiki/Skills), and [class overview](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.fandom.com/wiki/Class). The publisher’s [Book 2 sample, PDF pages 29–30](https://cdn.penguin.co.uk/dam-assets/books/9780241816219/9780241816219-sample.pdf) directly confirms that regular use, training, guilds, gear, potions and sponsors can improve skills, and that the Primal race unlocks training to level 20. The [secondary point-allocation summary](https://www.dungeoncrawlercarl.nl/en/news/the-five-player-stats-explained/) supplies the three-points-per-level detail. Exact game formulas and XP attribution remain adaptations.

**Book timing caution:** A chronological Book 1 campaign would begin before the protagonists can allocate points or pick a permanent race/class. The current demo combines early dungeon scenery with later party members and classes; it is explicitly a mixed-timeline adaptation. Katia's Doppelgänger and Monster Truck Driver choices happen later than the initial dungeon entry. A playable adaptation that starts with all those systems open is compressing progression for usability.

**Adaptation recommendation:** If keeping compact progression, say explicitly that crawler level awards a small stat-point pool, and skills unlock or level through use/rewards. Do not treat “skill level” and “character level” as synonyms. Use a small curated skill list rather than simulating all the novels' background skills. Do not give exact canonical stat-to-damage formulas; none are needed for a tactics game.

### Stances and combat identity

No source reference reviewed establishes a universal, named stance-selection system for the party. The novels show characters with recognizable combat approaches and class/race abilities, but a tactics UI's “stance” buttons are an adaptation.

Use stance as a plain tactical order (e.g. **Guard**, **Advance**, **Hold**) with deterministic effects, no lore claim. Character identity should come from source-grounded behavior: Carl improvises with explosives/traps and unarmed combat; Donut uses magic and her public persona; Katia changes shape and has a momentum/toughness class direction. The Book 2 publisher sample anchors Carl's Compensated Anarchist, Donut's Former Child Actor, and her learned Magic Missile; these identities can inform new game abilities without asserting exact book combat numbers.

### Safe rooms, crafting, equipment, pets

**Canon / secondary-source supported:** Safe rooms are protected service spaces. Crawlers need one to open loot boxes; after class/race selection, they also need one to allocate stored stat points. They can offer recovery, food, information, markets, or other services, but the exact facilities vary. A boss entering does not make it a normal battle arena: an attacking mob is expelled by the room's protections. Loot is awarded through several channels, including achievement, quest, boss, and sponsor/viewer boxes. See [safe rooms](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.fandom.com/wiki/Safe_Rooms) and [loot boxes](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.fandom.com/wiki/Loot_Boxes).

Crafting exists, but a universal crafting bench in every safe room is not supported. Simple combinations can be improvised in inventory; advanced crafting uses specialized workbenches in an appropriate location such as a safe room, guildhall, or personal space. Equipment may come from boxes, enemies, markets, or crafting; equipping it is separate from merely possessing it. See [crafting](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.fandom.com/wiki/Crafting) and [inventory](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.fandom.com/wiki/Inventory).

Pets and pet-related rewards are canon. A notable Book 1 example: Carl's entry with a cat earns a pet reward box with an enhanced biscuit, which changes Donut's status. This does not mean every party member receives a combat pet or every pet works like a summoned unit. Keep pets as a companion/equipment/reward system in the adaptation, with a clear distinction between canon-inspired flavor and invented pet actions. See the [Book 1 achievement index](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.wiki/book-1/achievements/) and [Enhanced Pet Biscuit entry](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.fandom.com/wiki/Enhanced_Pet_Biscuit).

**Adaptation recommendation:** Let the safe room perform rest, loadout changes, and reward opening as a reliable game checkpoint. Make crafting a limited recipe system available at a designated workshop/camp upgrade, not an assumed universal safe-room service. Equipment bonuses should be simple, explicit, and reversible. Pets can grant a support action or passive perk, clearly marked as gameplay invention.

### System AI and achievements

**Canon / secondary-source supported:** The System AI issues notifications, including achievement titles/descriptions/rewards. Achievements can celebrate, reward, mock, or warn crawlers. The tone is theatrical and snide, and is part of the reality-show satire. The [achievement overview](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.fandom.com/wiki/Achievement) and [Book 1 achievement index](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.wiki/book-1/achievements/) establish the shape of the system.

**Adaptation recommendation:** Write fresh achievement names and short AI narration based on visible player actions. Keep the voice brisk, petty, and showbiz-aware, without lifting recognizable lines or achievement copy from the books. Example original line: “NEW ACHIEVEMENT: CONSIDERED THE DOOR. The door remains unimpressed. Reward pending.” Use audience rewards as optional flavor/rewards, not hidden mandatory score gates.

## Book 1 boss precedents for scenario design

All three examples below are Book 1 encounters, so they fit the stated spoiler boundary.

1. **Hoarder — protected hoard and minions.** Carl and Donut encounter the first neighborhood boss amid a defended stockpile; the fight includes minions emerging from the boss and begins with Carl using dynamite. Source: [DCC Database Hoarder entry](https://www.dccdatabase.com/wiki/hoarder/). **Adaptation:** a boss guarded by a few weak adds, with explosive/environmental hazards as optional counters. Do not reproduce exact prose, set-piece choreography, or numbers.
2. **Juicer — cramped gym brawler.** The Juicer is a troglodyte neighborhood boss in a gym-like room, a close-range threat encountered while preparing for the larger borough threat. Source: [DCC Database Juicer entry](https://www.dccdatabase.com/wiki/juicer/). **Adaptation:** a telegraphed charge/grab threat with props that create lane blockers or tactical cover. The telegraphs and grid interactions are new mechanics.
3. **Ball of Swine — collaborative engineering problem.** This is a rolling borough boss faced by a larger Meadow Lark group. The party builds an improvised defensive contraption (“Speedbump”) from available furniture/gear and uses traps and explosives. Source: [Fandom Ball of Swine entry](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.fandom.com/wiki/Ball_of_Swine). **Adaptation:** the boss pressures a barricade while the team gathers, places, or arms parts over turns. Do not claim this happened with the game's four-person party; the novel scenario includes a larger group.

Use these as structural inspirations: protect something, survive a brawler's pressure, and solve a cooperative construction puzzle. Only the first is a straightforward small-party boss template. Each adaptation should have a non-gimmick path to victory and make any special interaction legible before commitment.

## Practical feature checklist

| Feature | Canon anchor | Safe game adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| Katia selectable forms | Mass-conserving body/gear reshaping; Monster Truck Driver has a speed/momentum + Constitution direction (secondary detail). | Label forms as game presets. Respect mass/footprint and show explicit tactical tradeoffs. No copied abilities or canonical named-stat packages. |
| Stat benefits | Three stat points per crawler level; manual distribution waits until after Third Floor class/race selection. | Use a small point pool and plainly describe that the game compresses the unlock timing. |
| Skill levels | Tracked separately from crawler levels; reflect prior knowledge and improve through training/use or rewards; class/race/gear can modify. | Grant skills at authored milestones and level selected skills through use/rewards. Keep formulas invented and readable. |
| Character stances | No canonical stance-selection menu verified. | Tactical commands (Guard/Hold/Advance) with explicit, deterministic effects; label as game mechanics. |
| Safe rooms | Protected hubs, box opening, later stat allocation, variable services. | Reliable rest, inventory, and party/loadout checkpoint. |
| Crafting | Improvised simple combinations and specialized workbenches; not a bench in every safe room. | Small recipe list at an authored workshop; recipe outcomes and materials are invented. |
| Equipment | Boxes, loot, markets, crafting; possession and equipping are distinct. | Few transparent gear slots/bonuses; show changes before leaving camp. |
| Pets | Pet boxes/biscuits and pet support exist; Donut is the prominent early example. | Optional support passive/action, bounded by an explicit roster rule; avoid implying every crawler has a pet. |
| AI achievement notices | System awards and narrates achievements; tone may be mocking/showy. | Original, action-triggered notices with small optional rewards; no book lines or mandatory audience score. |
| Bosses | Hoarder, Juicer, Ball of Swine are Book 1 neighborhood/borough precedents. | Adapt encounter structure and themes, not exact dialogue, stats, or scene sequence. |

## Scope and uncertainty notes

- Treat all combat ranges, turn order, stance names, selectable forms, stat modifiers, recipes, item numbers, pet actions, XP thresholds, and level-up cadence in the game as **adaptation**, unless separately supported by its implementation documentation.
- Source mechanics are reported from secondary summaries unless a publisher sample link is explicitly identified. Chapter references on fan pages are locator aids, not a substitute for checking the full text.
- Katia's exact book/chapter first-use timing for each shape and the full list of legal transformations remain uncertain from these sources. The game should not imply it is exhaustive.
- Keep plot references and characterization within Books 1–4. In particular, do not use later-series revelations as explanations for early systems or as art requirements.
