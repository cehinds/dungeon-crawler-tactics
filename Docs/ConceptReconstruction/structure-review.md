## What a reconstruction can claim

The inventory distinguishes source-visible objects from any inferred underpainting. An exact mask can preserve all visible pixels belonging to a separable feature, including its antialiased boundary. It cannot reveal portions behind another object. For example, the floor behind a character, the wall behind a pipe, or the tile underneath the HUD is absent from the evidence. A clean plate there must be painted or procedurally synthesized and must be labeled as reconstructed. It cannot be described as an exact extraction.

The whole image is not an acceptable layer. The image can be retained as a comparison/reference, but every delivered element must correspond to a semantic feature or a clearly named reconstructed surface. In particular, do not mask out or bake together the complete dungeon, complete HUD, or entire character/UI composite and then count that as layer extraction.

## Procedural process for a similar composition

1. **Establish the canvas and regions.** Use the 1536 × 1024 source coordinate system. Lay down the dark outer masonry and define the floor playfield. Keep wall bricks and floor slabs as separate features. The floor's mean pitch is about 75 px, but individual seams vary; trace the visible mortar landmarks rather than impose a fixed square grid.
2. **Build the room from reusable rules.** Trace visible wall seams from the source and draw the resulting source texture fragments with cool blue edge light and dark joints. The scene map defines 19 visible wall zones with 676 authored brick cells in the 19-zone mortar landmark map; 659 of those have nonempty visible masks in `scene-regions.json`, alongside 380 supplemental source-texture fragments. Manual landmarks group visible source texture according to visible brick seams; the supplemental masks preserve detailed source surface patches. These are source-visible masks, not recovered original editable brick art, and they cannot recover hidden faces. Trace floor slabs to visible source mortar boundaries and add per-slab cracks, chips and mottled texture. Add water pools/channels, grate insets, pipes, moss, ledges and gold light flecks as distinct overlays.
3. **Place architectural landmarks and props.** Center the exit doorway and arrow above the playable floor, flank it with torches, then place the visible waterfalls, grates, crates and explosive barrel using the recorded locations. Preserve the asymmetry and broken boundaries rather than tiling a uniform room.
4. **Place character sprites on board cells.** Use separate ally and enemy sprites with distinct silhouettes, face direction and the relative size shown in the source. Add four green ally bars and three red enemy bars as independent UI pieces. A similar scene may change poses or identities, but should retain the four-ally/three-rat arrangement if it claims to reproduce this exact composition.
5. **Draw tactical state over the board.** Put Carl's yellow selected tile beneath his sprite, the three cyan movement cells alongside it, then red target brackets and chevron over the center rat. Add the yellow-tailed “Strike • 2 Mana” callout. These are separate state overlays, not floor paint.
6. **Compose the HUD with reusable frames.** Make the party portrait cards and command controls from repeated dark panels with gold beveled borders, while preserving the brighter selected states. Keep labels and values as independent text; use the exact source transcription where reproducing this screen. The large bottom panel, four portraits, four action buttons, forecast and turn button retain the bounds/order in the inventory.
7. **Separate source masks from newly drawn pixels.** Preserve masks for visible features where they can be cleanly separated. Where features touch or overlap, record the shared edge/occlusion and avoid claiming invisible pixels. Create a separately named reconstructed clean plate only if the composition needs one; note which holes were invented and which visible pixels it supports.
